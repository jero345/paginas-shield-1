The date that matters

1 July 2027

If your adviser's business has collapsed, the Compensation Scheme of Last Resort can pay an AFCA award up to $150,000. The Government has announced that for complaints made after 30 June 2027 the Scheme is to pay only actual losses, not the returns you would otherwise have earned. Lodging before then keeps your full claim open.

Source: Treasury fact sheet, 18 August 2026. The change has been announced but is not yet law.

When our help is worth having

Some complaints are not straightforward

These are the situations where a lawyer is most likely to change the outcome for First Guardian investors. If one of them applies to you, ask us for a free assessment.

01You invested through a Diversa fund

Members of Diversa-trusteed funds, such as Australian Practical Super and the Powerwrap, MAP and Praemium options, have not been repaid. ASIC is suing Diversa.

02You invested through NQ Super

Equity Trustees members who invested in First Guardian have not been repaid. ASIC is suing the trustee, but that case will not be heard before 2027.

03Netwealth repaid your capital

You may still be owed the returns you would have earned in your old fund. We check the comparison AFCA uses.

04You were advised through InterPrac

Venture Egg and Rhys Reilly advised under InterPrac's licence. AFCA has paused decisions on InterPrac complaints while InterPrac's court case against AFCA runs.

05More than one adviser or platform

Every responsible firm needs to be identified and included in your complaint, or you may leave compensation behind.

06You used an SMSF

Your fund is the complainant, and the cost of winding up the SMSF can form part of the claim.

07Your loss is over $150,000, or you lost insurance

The Scheme's cap is $150,000, and life, TPD or trauma cover lost on the switch can be part of the claim. We look at what else can be recovered.

08You are in a class action or offered a deed

We check how the Netwealth class action affects your complaint, and whether any deed or settlement asks you to give up other rights, including rights against Diversa.

About our fees

What you are paying for

AFCA and the Compensation Scheme of Last Resort do not charge you. Our fee is for our work on your complaint, not for access to AFCA.

Where a complaint is complex, we will ask AFCA to order the financial firm to contribute to your legal costs. AFCA usually caps a contribution at $5,000 and does not always make one. Any contribution reduces what you pay us.

We only take on a complaint where the likely compensation is well above our fee. Before you sign anything we will give you a written costs agreement and disclosure explaining the fee, when it is payable and your rights.

Common questions

Do I need a lawyer to complain to AFCA?

No. AFCA is free and designed to be used without a lawyer. We act only where your circumstances make our involvement worthwhile, and we will tell you if they do not.

I invested through a Diversa fund and have not been repaid.

You can complain to AFCA about your adviser now. ASIC's case against Diversa seeks compensation for members, but the trustee is defending it.

Netwealth repaid my money. Do I still have a claim?

Possibly. AFCA's approach is to put you where you would have been in your previous fund, which can include lost returns after allowing for the repayment.

My adviser was with Venture Egg or Rhys Reilly. What now?

They advised under InterPrac's licence. AFCA is still accepting and investigating InterPrac complaints, but will not make decisions until InterPrac's case against AFCA ends. Make your complaint now so it is in the queue.

Will I get anything from First Guardian itself?

The liquidators are pursuing recoveries but have warned investors to expect little. A complaint against your adviser does not depend on those recoveries.

How much will it cost me?

Nothing upfront, and nothing if your complaint does not succeed. If you receive compensation, our fixed fee is $5,000 including GST, paid from that compensation. AFCA itself is free.
